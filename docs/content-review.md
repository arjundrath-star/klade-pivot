# Content review checklist

For the teacher reviewer. Every session's problems are hand-written TypeScript in `src/content/`, graded by exact arithmetic, and never generated by a model. This file tracks what a teacher has checked and what is still a draft.

## What to check on every session

**Standard alignment.** The unit is Common Core HSA-REI.B.3: solve linear equations in one variable, including equations with coefficients represented by letters. Each session should practice one step of that standard and nothing it does not teach.

**Realism.** Every word problem has one equation and seven framings: sports, music, gaming, food, creators, animals, and a neutral one. The numbers are drawn at random from ranges, and every framing has to make sense for every number those ranges can produce, to a kid who knows the topic. No 11-goal soccer games, no 200-song albums, no $2 concert tickets. Each range has a comment saying what it counts, and `tests/unit/content/` fails if a range grows past the limit written there, so widening one means reading all seven framings again.

**Reading level.** Short sentences in everyday words, at or below an 8th-grade reading level, since students start in grade 6. The test caps the lesson at 150 words and an average of 15 words per sentence. Word problems should be readable aloud in one breath per sentence.

**Math.** Every problem has one integer answer. Word problems use positive numbers only; symbolic problems may use negatives. The worked example's steps are the standard inverse-operation order a teacher would show.

**Tone and fit.** Nothing a parent would object to, no brands, no gendered pronouns for adults in the story, and no framing that assumes money or access a family may not have.
